# M-20261015-05

- **Evidence:** `ps` at 12:04Z: `bash …/history.sh dotfiles-conformance  100000` (pid 58660) alive for 55:33 under `dispatch.sh … rs8-review-line-r4.txt`; the bus held no round-4 line until 12:06:03Z; `history.sh <team> \"\" 500` took 4.1 s and `\"\" 1000` took 390 s on the same team (1385 rows, `messages.db` 3.4 MB); the seat's PONG of 12:08:37Z: the line's hashes were round 4 while the files on disk were round 5.
- **Detection and why not earlier:** dispatch.sh's round-2 send of 10:59Z had taken about three minutes end to end, so the round-4 job's silence was read as the same slowness; the job's output file is written only at the end, and nothing bounded the wait.
- **Root cause:** upstream `history.sh` (lines 57 and 100) doubles the quotes of the whole JSON slice with bash parameter substitution (`${_arr//'/''}`) before feeding it to sqlite; under macOS `/bin/bash` 3.2.57 that substitution is superlinear in the slice's size (a hundred-fold time for a two-fold slice), and dispatch.sh reads the whole bus (`BUS_READ_LIMIT` 100000) to count identical rows before and after each send.
- **Should have been:** the send path needs the last few bus rows, not the whole history: the pre-send count of identical rows and the post-send read marker both come from a bounded read (the last 8 rows suffice while one sender sends one line at a time), and a read that outlives a bound is a refusal, not a wait.
- **Corrections:** C-53.
