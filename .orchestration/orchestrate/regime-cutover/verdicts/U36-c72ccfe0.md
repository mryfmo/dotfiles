## F2 (P1, confidence high): the shared lock is stolen by age alone

`scripts/orchestrator/regime-start:129-136` (`take_lock`) removes the lock directory when its mtime is older than ten minutes without checking that its owner is dead; `:259-261` touches it once before `hcom-seat` and never during that blocking call or the wait loop, and `REGIME_START_WAIT_SECONDS` admits 9999 s. A slow or paused launch can lose its lock to `regime-stop`, which removes the registration and returns; the launch then completes and recreates it, so the original race survives. The unconditional `trap 'rmdir "${lock}"' EXIT` can also remove a replacement holder's lock.

Required fix: process-owned locking. The lock directory records its owner (`pid` and a start-time or boot-id marker that a reused pid cannot forge); recovery only when that owner is verified dead (`kill -0` fails and the marker matches), never by age; release only by the owner (compare the marker before `rmdir`); the hold spans the whole launch and wait without needing refreshes. Tests: extend the paused-relaunch case by ageing the lock with `os.utime` while its owner is alive and prove stop cannot overtake it and that no seat or registration survives a completed stop; a dead-owner recovery case without a live launch continuing outside the lock; an ownership-checked release case (a second holder's lock survives the first process's exit).
